Having just taken delivery of your first 5-axis Okuma mill, three things need sorting to accelerate commissioning; a machine simulation configuration you can trust, a post that outputs code your control is happy with and the Fusion toolpath knowledge to drive it. Here's how I'd suggest getting there in a cost effective and sustainable way.

1Machine simulation setup in Fusion

Okuma have already handed you the 3D model, which saves a solid chunk of work. What it won't be is ready to use. Those models arrive loaded with fixture detail, fasteners and cosmetic geometry that'll bog Fusion down.

What I'll do:

  • Simplify and prepare the Okuma model for use in Fusion
  • Build the machine definition, with kinematics and travel limits taken from your Okuma documentation
  • Configure the collision bodies that matter
  • Validate the finished definition against a known collision

I'll clean up and configure your vices, chucks and fixtures so they're usable in simulation, working from models you supply. $125 per setup. Tell me how many you want covered up front, or we can do them as they come up.

2Five-axis post processor

I'll start from the Autodesk post for your control.

Configuration covers rotary axis output and limits, linearisation tolerance, safe retract and repositioning moves, work offset handling, coolant and canned cycle mapping (where supported), and the program header to suit your shop.

Post work is the least predictable part of the job. The base configuration is quick. What eats time is the back and forth once you're actually cutting, because every shop turns up edge cases the base post has never met. So I've scoped it as a base configuration plus three rounds of changes after initial delivery, each one worked off your feedback with test programs to prove it. Past that, we agree more time.

Machine-side testing is done by your operator once the machine is commissioned. I work from the feedback and results that come back off those runs. If you want me on site to support the proving, travel and expenses are chargeable on top.

Any custom requirements relating to robot integration sit outside this. I'd need to assess those separately and would likely quote them on their own.

The post is supplied unencrypted and fully version controlled via GitHub, so you can read it, see exactly what changed between revisions, and roll back if you need to.

$2,750 Including 3 x 1 hour revision rounds after initial delivery.

3Advanced Fusion CAM training for simultaneous five-axis

Four half-day modules.

Module 1: Setting up for multi-axis

Tool orientation and work coordinate setup for five-axis, then retract and linking strategy. A lot of multi-axis crashes happen around linking and repositioning moves, so knowing how to control them matters.

Module 2: The toolpath catalogue

5-axis controls in 3D toolpaths, multi-axis clearing, advanced swarf, and multi-axis finishing. The focus is on picking the right strategy for the job in front of you.

Module 3: Surface modelling to drive toolpaths

Understanding surface topology, building drive and check surfaces, patch and loft techniques for geometry that exists purely to steer the cutter, guide curves and boundary surfaces to control tilt and lead-lag. Most of the control you get over a five-axis toolpath comes from geometry you model yourself.

Module 4: Your parts, start to finish

We program a real job of yours end to end, verify it in the machine simulation, post it, and prove it.

Send me two or three representative parts ahead of time, ideally the ones you've already had a go at and got stuck on. I'll provide baseline datasets as well so we're not stalled if your parts turn out to be awkward teaching examples.

Nobody can promise you'll be five-axis experts after only two days of training. What you'll have is an understanding of the underlying concept of how these toolpaths work, a working method for picking the right strategy, and the means to prove it in simulation before it goes near the machine.

The four modules are quoted as a block, not individually. They build on each other and module four leans on the first three, so cherry-picking one or two would leave you short.

$3,600 Four half-days at an $1,800 day rate.
